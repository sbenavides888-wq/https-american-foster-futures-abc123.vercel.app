National Movement for Foster Care Tuition Waivers

Every foster youth deserves tuition-free access to public colleges. We are building a nationwide movement to replicate Texas' model and establish cross-state reciprocity.

The Goal

Texas offers one of the nation's strongest foster youth tuition waiver programs. Our movement works state-by-state to pass similar laws — and to honor waivers across state lines through reciprocity.

Policy case

Why Texas-style tuition waivers outperform the federal Chafee grant

Texas-style tuition and fee waivers give foster youth a stronger path through higher education than the federal Chafee Education and Training Voucher alone. Chafee tops out at $5,000 a year—an amount that has not kept pace with college costs and is often reduced, delayed, or unavailable when state funding runs short. Many states also prioritize students already receiving the voucher or already enrolled, so first-time applicants can wait in line. By contrast, a Texas-style waiver eliminates 100% of tuition and required fees at public institutions for eligible current and former foster youth. It is a clear statutory benefit, not a competitive grant that can run out mid-year.

Removing the largest fixed educational cost gives students real predictability and lets other aid—Pell Grants, state grants, and Chafee when available—stretch further for housing, books, and living expenses. For young people who already face housing instability and interrupted schooling, a full tuition waiver is more powerful than a limited federal voucher. Chafee remains a useful supplement; it should not be the primary foundation. States serious about college outcomes for foster youth should put robust tuition and fee waivers first—and use improved Chafee funding only to fill remaining gaps.
