Product brief · iOS & Android

AFF Futures

The pocket path from care to campus — and the campaign phone that puts foster youth at the center of winning tuition waivers, state by state.

Owner: American Foster Futures
Platforms: iOS + Android (one codebase)
Primary user: Foster youth 16–23
Also serves: Mentors, volunteers, allies

Success condition for this brief: A young person can open the app with almost no friction, see their state, access education and transition services, join the national tuition-waiver movement, take action on their state campaign, and invite adults to volunteer or help — without leaving the product for “another website.”

1. Problem & opportunity

Today
  • Resources live on a static website; youth rarely complete multi-page journeys on mobile browsers under stress.
  • ~23,000 youth age out yearly; many miss tuition waivers, FAFSA, and housing steps until it is too late.
  • National and state campaigns need voices of people with lived experience — not only professional advocates.
  • Volunteers and helpers have no single place to sign up, get trained lightly, and support a youth or a bill.
With AFF Futures
  • One app = services + campaign + community of support, personalized by state.
  • Checklists, deadlines, and secure document tips turn “read this guide” into “do the next step.”
  • Youth can join national and state campaign actions in two taps (call, email, share, testify interest).
  • Adults convert to volunteers, mentors, or donors without a separate funnel.

2. Users & roles

Primary · 70% product focus
Youth

Ages ~16–23, in care or aged out. Need services, deadlines, waiver path, and optional campaign voice. May have limited data, unstable housing, low trust of institutions.

Secondary
Helper

Mentor, caseworker ally, former foster peer, family friend. Helps with checklists (with youth consent), walks FAFSA/waiver steps, attends hearings with youth.

Tertiary
Ally / Volunteer

Public supporter: volunteer, testify, share bills, donate, staff events. Not always connected to a specific youth.

3. How foster youth access the app (meaningful onboarding)

Friction kills adoption. Onboarding must work for someone who has 90 seconds, a shared phone, or no email.

1
Discover

QR on outreach cards · link from website “Get the app” · TikTok/IG · school liaison · group home partner · text short link after hearing or event. App Store / Play Store listing: “Foster youth college & aging-out tools + tuition waiver campaign.”

2
Open without a full account (Browse mode)

Immediate access to: national map of waivers, state resource list, aging-out checklist preview, crisis/hotline strip. No forced signup wall. Optional later: “Save my progress” with phone OTP or email.

3
Pick my state (and optional “I lived in care here”)

One question drives the whole product: Which state are you in now? Optional: care history state(s). Unlocks state campaign status, local waiver rules, housing links, and “your bill” actions.

4
Choose “what I need right now”

Chips: College money · Aging out · Housing · Job/career · Fight for waivers · Talk to someone. Routes into the right hub without a tutorial video.

5
Home hub = My path + My campaign

Always show: next 1–3 action cards (e.g. “Start FAFSA,” “Check Texas waiver eligibility,” “Your state has no comprehensive waiver — send this message to your rep”). Campaign and services share the same home so advocacy never feels separate from survival needs.

5. Services access (education & transition)

Services are not a PDF library. Each area is a guided path with steps, links, and “I’m stuck — message AFF.”

Educational opportunities
  • College money path: independent student FAFSA → state waiver → Chafee ETV → stack aid (mirrors site guide).
  • Waiver eligibility quiz: age/care history → “likely eligible / need verification / no program yet — join campaign.”
  • School search tips: public colleges that accept waiver; foster youth campus liaison prompt.
  • Request education support: form → AFF education navigation queue.
  • Reminders: FAFSA open date, state deadlines, re-file yearly.
Aging out & independence
  • Interactive checklist (start from Texas model; expand state packs): IDs, education, housing, health, benefits.
  • Progress % and “share checklist with helper” (consent required).
  • Housing 18–23: program types + state links + “need housing help” request.
  • Employment & career: first-job basics, resume tips, AFF employment pathway content.
  • Optional later: Foundation Program learning modules (AI literacy etc.) as “bonus path,” not blocking core.
Service request loop: Youth taps “I need help with ___” → short form (state, need type, contact) → AFF staff/volunteer dashboard (web) → response in-app or SMS. This is how the app becomes a real service channel, not only content.

6. National campaign + relevant state campaign

National layer (always on)

Movement for Foster Care Tuition Waivers

  • One-screen “why it matters” + national map (waiver / no comprehensive waiver).
  • Goal progress: states won / remaining (aligned with Case for Support planning model).
  • Actions: share campaign card · follow AFF social · donate · sign national petition/list.
  • Youth story opt-in: “I used a waiver / I need a waiver” (consent + safety review before publish).
  • Training lite: 5-minute “how to talk about tuition waivers” for youth advocates.
State layer (from onboarding state)

My state campaign desk

  • Status card: Strong waiver (model) · Partial/multilayer · No comprehensive program (campaign focus).
  • If campaign state: bill status (when known), talking points, email/call script to legislators, event RSVP, “want to testify” interest form.
  • If implementation year: “Law passed — how to use it” checklist (matches AFF office model: campaign → implement → redeploy).
  • If strong waiver state (e.g. Texas): shift to usage guide + peer mentoring + support other states.
  • Local volunteer needs: “phone bank Saturday,” “research help,” “ride to hearing.”
Youth action (2 taps)

Pre-written SMS/email to legislator with name + ZIP; youth can edit. Tracks “actions taken” privately for impact stories.

Ally action

Same tools without care history; prompts to volunteer or fund state office slot.

Safety default

Public posts and media contact always opt-in; default is private action + private service requests.

8. End-to-end scenarios (must all work)

Scenario A · Youth needs college money

Install → Browse → State = Texas → “College money” → FAFSA step complete → Waiver guide → Save progress with phone OTP → Reminder set → Optional: “Share with helper.”

Scenario B · Youth in no-waiver state joins campaign

State = Alabama (example focus) → Campaign tab shows “No comprehensive waiver” → Read 3 talking points → Send legislator email → RSVP virtual training → Offer to share story (opt-in) → See national map of where wins are building.

Scenario C · Ally becomes volunteer

Open app from Case for Support QR → Role = Ally → Get Involved → Volunteer → Policy research + phone bank → Confirm → Receives “next campaign shift” notification for their state.

Scenario D · Youth needs housing + education together

Home shows both cards → Housing request form + College path in parallel → Staff sees combined need → Youth gets single reply thread in Me → Campaign action not required but offered: “Stable housing + tuition free college is the goal of our movement.”

9. MVP scope vs later

MVP (ship this first)
  • Browse mode + state picker + Home next actions
  • College money path (full content from site)
  • Aging-out checklist (Texas pack + generic national pack)
  • Campaign: national map + state status + email/call scripts + action log
  • Volunteer signup + education/housing “request help” forms
  • Donate deep-link to Zeffy
  • Push notifications for deadlines & campaign moments
  • Privacy policy, crisis resources always visible
Later
  • Encrypted document vault
  • Live bill tracking API integrations
  • Mentor matching (after human screening workflow)
  • Multi-language (Spanish first)
  • Foundation Program modules
  • Full offline mode; multi-state care history profiles
  • Staff admin app with case queues & campaign CRM

10. Success metrics & technology

North-star metrics
  • % of new users who complete state selection + first service step in session 1
  • Checklist steps completed / month
  • Campaign actions (emails/calls/shares) by state
  • Volunteer applications submitted via app
  • Service requests closed with response time
  • Return rate: youth who open app again within 14 days
Recommended stack
  • Client: Flutter or React Native (iOS + Android)
  • Or phase 0: PWA installable from americanfosterfutures.org for speed
  • Backend: Supabase or Firebase (auth OTP, forms, content CMS)
  • Content: Sync structured content from website programs; single source of truth for waiver status by state
  • Safety: minimal data; no sale of data; youth content moderation queue for public stories

11. Design principles (non-negotiable)

Dignity first

No pity imagery language; peer tone; lived-experience leadership visible.

Low friction

Browse without account; large taps; works on slow phones.

Services + power

Always pair help with a path to change the system.

Safety

Crisis bar; opt-in visibility; consent before any share.

12. Recommendation (decision line)

Build AFF Futures as a dual-purpose mobile product: a service companion for education, aging out, housing, and employment pathways, and a campaign handset for the national tuition-waiver movement with a live desk for each user’s state. Volunteer and donor conversion lives in the same app so “get help,” “give help,” and “change the law” reinforce each other.
