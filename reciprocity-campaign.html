Tuition Waiver Reciprocity Campaign

Allow an individual with a tuition waiver in one state to access the benefits of a similar waiver in another state.

Alongside the National Campaign for Foster Youth Tuition Waivers, American Foster Futures advances a dedicated Reciprocity Waiver Campaign. Drawn from AFF’s final business plan, this work is a core part of our policy agenda.

Objective

Establish or expand tuition waivers for foster youth in all 50 states, ensuring equitable access to higher education and vocational training—a critical step toward breaking the cycle of poverty.

AFF drives legislative change by engaging policymakers and stakeholders on the introduction and passage of legislation in states with no waiver and the improvement or expansion of programs already in place. Additionally, AFF seeks state-by-state reciprocity, thereby allowing an individual with a waiver in one state the ability to access the benefits of a similar waiver in another state.

Why reciprocity matters

  • Foster youth move across state lines for college, work, family, and specialized programs—education benefits should not stop at a border.
  • Without reciprocity, a youth who earned a waiver in one state may lose that pathway when they transfer or enroll out of state.
  • Reciprocity pairs with AFF’s Texas-style waiver campaign: expand strong programs and make them portable.

How we work

  • Advocacy and lobbying strategy — engage legislators and stakeholders to introduce, pass, and improve waiver laws, including reciprocity language where possible.
  • Evidence-based materials — research, reports, and recommendations that show why portable tuition support improves enrollment and completion.
  • Grassroots organizing — pilot and expand state campaigns, including states with no waiver and states whose systems need improvement.

Source: Business Plan — American Foster Futures (FINAL). Program details and sequencing may evolve with capacity and legislative calendars.
