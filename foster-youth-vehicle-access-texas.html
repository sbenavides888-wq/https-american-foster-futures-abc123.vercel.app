Foster Youth Priority Vehicle Access Program

Eligible foster youth ages 18–22 get first chance, after other state agencies, to buy surplus vehicles Texas already sells to the public — at a modest discount, with a program administrator.

See how a sale works Download lawmaker brief

This is not a new fleet, a loan program, or a change in when an agency retires a vehicle. It changes who stands in line and at what price. Texas already owns the cars and already runs the sale. Moving verified youth ahead of the public auction — after other state agencies, and before cities, counties, and assistance organizations — is the smallest statutory change that puts those cars to work for the youth the state just raised.

The proposal

Line. Price. Administrator.

Chapter 2175

Amend who stands in line

File as a surplus-property amendment for the 2027 session. After other state agencies, a certified eligible youth may take a posted passenger vehicle before political subdivisions and the public auction.

Existing sales

Use the surplus Texas already sells

TFC sets the list price. Eligible buyers pay that price minus a discount set in the General Appropriations Act (capped at 50%). Retirement rules, university surplus, and DPS law-enforcement transfers stay as written.

No new car desk

A program administrator

The program administrator matches verified youth to posted vehicles, pays for as-is inspection, sits at close, and reports each September 30 — so TFC, TxDOT, DFPS, and TPWD do not have to stand up a new car desk.

Why it matters

Aging out without a car is a practical crisis

“As a former ward of the State of Texas, I can tell you that aging out of care without a car means missed shifts in bad weather, missed class, missed medical appointments, and missed childcare.”

Stephen Benavides, Founder and Executive Director, American Foster Futures
School & training

Young adults have a better chance of making class, internships, and campuses — especially in a state as vast as Texas.

Work

Shift jobs, early hours, and bad weather all require a way to get there. Missing transportation can mean missing paychecks.

Independence

Medical appointments, childcare, and housing all depend on getting there on time. Reliable transportation is a foundation for life after care.

Who can buy

Certified eligible youth, ages 18–22

DFPS verifies eligibility through a limited data match with the program administrator. If no eligible youth closes during the youth window, the vehicle sells under current law.

Care history

  • Was in DFPS conservatorship
  • Age 18 through 22
  • In or has aged out of foster care, extended foster care, or Preparation for Adult Living (PAL)

Ready to close

  • Valid Texas driver’s license
  • Active liability insurance
  • Completed a two-hour program administrator-taught financial literacy and vehicle-responsibility course
  • Shows the funds to close

Passenger vehicles only: car, SUV, van, or light truck with a GVWR of 10,000 pounds or less. Units that still carry unremoved emergency equipment are ineligible until the disposing agency certifies the equipment has been stripped.

Proposed order of sale

Youth go second — not first

Another state agency that still needs the unit always comes first. Priority access must attach to every Chapter 2175 disposal method used for passenger vehicles — TFC storefront, delegated agency yard, and internet auction — not only Austin walk-up sales.

  1. 1

    Other state agencies

    A state agency that still needs the unit takes it first. Youth never jump an agency that still needs the vehicle.

  2. 2

    Certified eligible youth

    Ages 18–22. Title transfers from the disposing agency to the youth. The program administrator does not take title.

  3. 3

    Cities, counties & assistance orgs

    Political subdivisions and other assistance organizations, as under current surplus law.

  4. 4

    Public auction

    If no eligible youth closes during the youth window, the vehicle sells under current law.

How a sale works

From posted list to keys in hand

Operations, not statute. The disposing agency titles directly to the youth. The program administrator sits at close but does not take title, warehouse vehicles, or set the state price.

1

Apply and verify

The youth applies to the program administrator. DFPS confirms eligibility through a limited data match (name, date of birth, and care-history flag) with a two-business-day turnaround. The program administrator does not certify status on its own.

2

Get ready to close

The buyer completes the program administrator’s financial-responsibility and vehicle-ownership course, and shows a valid driver’s license, active insurance, and the ability to finalize the transaction.

3

Match to a posted vehicle

The program administrator watches TFC and delegated-agency posting channels and alerts matched youth in the region by website and phone app. During the statutory 10-business-day window, an eligible youth signals interest. If two or more eligible youth want the same vehicle, the program administrator applies published selection rules (region, license and insurance ready, funds in hand, first complete file, hardship) and documents the choice.

4

Inspect, close, title to the youth

Vehicles remain as-is from the State. Independent inspection is the program administrator’s cost. If the vehicle is not safely operable, it returns to the disposing agency. If it passes and the youth can close, TFC or the delegated agency titles directly to the youth. Distance is the youth’s choice: the State and the program administrator do not fund transport.

5

Follow up and report

The program administrator confirms insurance at day 30 and files one report each September 30 with TFC, DFPS, and the Legislature. TFC storefront title packets take approximately 21 days; that gap is built into insurance and close planning.

Who does what

Agencies keep the sale. The program administrator runs the match.

TFC, TxDOT, and other disposing agencies

Does

Set the official list price. Hold the vehicle. Close title. Run auctions and delegated yard sales.

Does not

Certify foster status. Take walk-up “foster youth” calls. Coordinate with the buyer before or after sale.

Program administrator

Does

Verify eligibility after the DFPS data match. Match a youth to a posted vehicle. Pay for as-is inspection out of operating funds. Help assemble funds and sit at close. Report each September 30.

Does not

Set the state price. Run the auction. Take title. Take a lien. Warehouse a vehicle. Take a vehicle another agency still needs.

DFPS

Does

Confirm care history through a limited data match (name, DOB, care-history flag) within two business days.

Does not

Run a separate car desk.

Guardrails

What this bill does not do

  • Does not put a youth ahead of HHSC, DPS, TxDOT, or any agency that still needs the vehicle.
  • Does not touch university surplus (§ 2175.304) or rewrite DPS transfers to local law enforcement (§ 2175.308).
  • Does not make the program administrator a lender, auctioneer, the State’s pricing desk, a title holder, or a warehouse.
  • Does not add a second statutory hold on top of the existing 10-business-day posting window.
Program cost

Discount in the GAA. Proceeds stay in the surplus path.

TFC sets the list price. Eligible buyers pay that price minus a discount set in the General Appropriations Act, capped at 50%. Opening ask is 50 percent; the Legislature can negotiate down to 20–30 percent after volume is scored. Collected sale proceeds stay with owning agencies and General Revenue — not a direct payment to the program administrator.

Discount
GAA cap 50%

Do not hard-code 50% in statute. Place the discount and year-1 pilot cost in the GAA after LBB has a volume estimate.

Year-1 pilot
$585,000 · 5 FTEs

One executive director and four regional coordinators in high-volume DFPS regions: DFW, Houston, Austin, San Antonio, and El Paso. The program administrator does not warehouse vehicles.

Illustration only
Not a scored fiscal note

Planning figures for forgone surplus revenue and a possible revenue-recycling rider are in the lawmaker brief.

Model legislation

Based on Louisiana HB 575 / Act 436

The model bill passed in Louisiana in mid-2026 as HB 575 / Act 436 with virtually no opposition. It uses existing public property systems — not a new fleet or a giveaway from scratch.

  • Texas would amend Chapter 2175 so the same youth window applies to TFC storefront, delegated yard sales, and auctions-in-place.
  • Preferred hold rule: waive or shorten the two-year anti-flip hold to 12 months when title is in the name of a certified eligible youth.
  • University surplus and DPS law-enforcement transfers stay as written.
View Louisiana HB 575
Ask of the 2027 session

File as a Chapter 2175 amendment

  • Amend §§ 2175.184, 2175.065, and 2175.186–.187; add new § 2175.1845 (eligibility, notice, GAA price rule, program administrator, report).
  • Youth window: 10 business days for interest and 10–15 business days to close, including a fallback if the 10-day window ends mid-close.
  • Place the discount and year-1 pilot administration cost in the GAA. If the Legislature recycles revenue, reappropriate a capped slice to the program administrator; do not pay the program administrator directly from the sale.
  • Meanwhile, the program administrator can complete a TFC assistance-organization filing so it can match youth to posted cars under current § 2175.184 while the bill is pending. The program administrator still does not take title or warehouse vehicles.
